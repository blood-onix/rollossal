## R-01: Constitution amendment comes first

**Status checked**: `.specify/memory/constitution.md` is v3.0.0 (Last Amended 2026-11-01). A search for "visual gate",
"proper-noun" and any CC0 language finds nothing. The 2026-11-02 owner decisions are not in it, and nothing under
`docs/` records them either. FR-001 says that if it is not amended the plan must require the amendment first.

**Decision**: delivery row 0 is `/speckit-constitution`, merged by PR with owner approval. Probable bump: MINOR (3.1.0),
because it adds and relaxes rules without removing a principle. Wording to add:

- **Principle IV** (replace "words on textures (pictograms only)"): invented proper-noun shop names and short signage
  are allowed on textures and billboards. Real brands, logos, flags, meme images, real faces, alcohol or gambling
  names, and the words "Roblox", "Robux" and "Blox" stay banned.
- **Principle I and Quality Gates**: add a *visual gate*. The five fixed shots are mandatory for any map or art
  feature and are exempt from the two-failure rule. The owner's review of the shots, and the owner playing the build,
  CAN hold a map or art feature. All non-visual owner checks (phone fps, taste on pacing) stay on
  `docs/release-candidate.md` and still MUST NOT hold a feature.
- **Principle XI**: a CC0 mesh imported and moved onto the theme atlas in Blender counts as "made or edited in Blender",
  with its licence recorded. One shared facade/sign atlas and a capped set of billboard textures are allowed alongside
  the 1,024 px theme atlas. Creator Store models with scripts stay forbidden; mesh-only models are allowed after
  inspection.

**What the gap blocks**: authoring signage, importing a CC0 mesh into the game, and treating the visual gate as binding.
**What it does not block**: the camera set, the capture route, the "before" set, drafting the bible, the benchmark,
the bake-off comparison renders (nothing is shipped from them).

**Rationale**: Governance says agents MUST flag conflicts in the plan and amendments go through a PR. Silently
following the spec over the constitution is not allowed; silently following the constitution would drop the owner's
decision.

**Alternatives**: amend inside this feature's own tasks (rejected: it is a governance change that the owner approves
separately, and the plan must not self-approve); proceed and note the conflict (rejected by FR-001).

## R-02: Camera definitions shared by both capture paths

**Decision**: `art/capture/cameras.json` holds five shots, each with `name`, `diameter`, `position [x,y,z]`,
`lookAt [x,y,z]`, `fov` (70), `theme` (`city`), and `lighting` (taken from `Themes.city`: brightness 3, clockTime 13,
fog 200 to 1000, atmosphere 0.25). Distances follow the in-game rule `Movement.cameraDistance(d) = min(320, max(8,
4·d))`, so D2 → 8, D5 → 20, D12 → 48, D30 → 120, D80 → 320 (`03_midgame_d12_30` carries both a D12 and a D30 pose in
one shot name; the manifest records the diameter used, D12 as the default and D30 optional). Pitch stays inside the
80° `MAX_PITCH`.

| Shot | Ball at | Diameter | Camera idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `01_spawn_d2` | a lane spawn (slot 1) | 2 | behind and above the ball, looking along the lane |
| `02_street_d5` | main street | 5 | street level, facades both sides |
| `03_midgame_d12_30` | Ring 2/3 boundary | 12 | wider, parks and yards in frame |
| `04_lategame_d80` | Ring 4 boulevard | 80 | high pull-back, skyline visible |
| `05_overhead_map` | map centre | – | straight-down-ish, whole 880 stud city |

Exact positions are chosen at implementation from `Downtown.luau` spawn/zone data (the spawn list is 12 entries
generated by `turn(r, x, z)`; yaw is `atan2(-x,-z)`). The test pins names, FOV, theme, distance-by-diameter and the
pitch limit, not world coordinates, so a layout change does not silently invalidate the set.

**Rationale**: one file means the Studio path and the Blender path cannot drift. The shots mirror what a player sees
at the five growth stages, which is what the visual gate is meant to judge.

**Alternatives**: separate camera tables per path (rejected, drift); a Luau module (rejected, Blender cannot read it).

## R-03: Offline fallback renderer design

**Decision**: two parts, both headless and desktop-safe.

1. `scripts/capture-export.luau` (Lune) loads `Downtown.luau`, `Themes.luau`, `Layout` and `ThemeResolve` and calls
   `ThemeResolve.resolve(world, "city", Layout.expand, maxPlacements)`. It writes JSON: blocks (kind, cframe, size,
   swatch resolved to RGB through the environment palette over the theme palette), and placements (`objectId`,
   `assetKey`, x, y, z, yaw radians, object `size`). Sorted keys and fixed number formatting so output is
   byte-identical between runs.
2. `art/scripts/capture_render.py` runs in Blender through the MCP. It builds blocks as boxes/wedges/cylinders
   coloured from the palette, imports each referenced `art/export/dt_*.fbx` once and instances it, applies the same
   rule as `ObjectVisual.buildMesh` (longest side scaled to the object's `size`), places with
   `CFrame.new(x, y + size.Y/2, z) * Angles(0, yaw, 0)`, maps UVs onto `art/atlases/city.png`, sets sun and sky from the
   shot's lighting, and renders each camera at 1280×720.

FBX axes follow `export_all.py` (`axis_forward=-Z`, `axis_up=Y`); the importer must use the same conversion.

**Engine**: Blender's only engine here is EEVEE, plus Workbench. Use EEVEE for the committed shots (sun, shadows,
colour); Workbench with material colour is the fallback if EEVEE is unavailable headlessly. `art/scripts/qa_render.py`
already uses Workbench, which is the precedent for flat atlas colour.

**Parity limits, recorded in the capture manifest**: Roblox applies its own tone-mapping, bloom, atmosphere and
shadow filtering that Blender does not reproduce; instanced FBX scaling must match the in-game rule; invisible
boundary colliders are skipped. The offline set is a faithful *composition* check (shapes, colour hierarchy,
density, spacing) rather than a pixel match. The manifest says which path produced each image.

**Determinism**: no random in the renderer; fixed samples; the same inputs give the same pixels on the same Blender
build (version pinned in the manifest).

**Rationale**: the spec's primary path is Studio, but it needs Rojo synced and a throttle-free Studio. The offline path
runs even when those fail, which is the two-failure rule's reason for existing.

**Alternatives**: reimplementing layout in Python (rejected, second source of truth); exporting a glTF of the whole
city from Studio (rejected, no file export from the MCP).

## R-04: The "before" set

## R-05: Style bible structure and tier utilisation

**Decision**: `docs/design/08-style-bible.md`, one section per topic in the spec: colour hierarchy; proportions and edge
treatment; per-class and per-tier triangle floors and targets; facades, awnings and invented shop signs; roads,
lines, crossings, sidewalk and kerb; tree and lamp spacing, prop orientation; warm sun lighting; `city`-first with
`winter` as dressing; the ball-as-heap look; sourcing rule; override list. It opens with a short "this overrides
`06-art-style.md`" statement and lists what it replaces.

**Triangle floors and targets**: tier budgets are `{500, 700, 1500, 3500, 5000}`. Today's `dt_*` medians are
72 / 108 / 136 / 216 / 272, i.e. 14 % / 15 % / 9 % / 6 % / 5 % of budget. The bible sets, by tier, a floor of about
30 % of budget and a target of about 55 %, as *proposals to be adjusted by the bake-off* (a hatchback at 216 against
a 3,500 budget is the case the bake-off tests). Per-class overrides are allowed where a class cannot use its tier
(a hydrant is tier 2 at 2.4 studs; a 700 budget is not a target for a prop that small). Final numbers are written after
bake-off (delivery row 11). These are targets for *new* assets; the 164 existing meshes are not retro-fixed here.

**Rationale**: gives the owner a bar to say "this prop is too thin" against, with numbers. Mid-utilisation also gives
the sizing in R-14 (about 568k unculled / 1.04M triangles for a full budget at those bands; the benchmark decides what
the platform actually allows).

**Alternatives**: one global triangle target (rejected, tier budgets already differ by 10×); no numbers (rejected by
the spec).

## R-08: Bake-off stage and scoring

**Decision**: one Blender scene: neutral grey floor, one warm sun (same direction and strength as `city`), the same
camera for every candidate at 3/4 view and a second at game-camera distance for the prop's tier (so thin detail is
judged at the scale a player sees it). Props shown at the longest-side size from `Downtown.luau` (hydrant 2.4, bench
7.0, street tree 9.0, hatchback 14, `shop_front` ≈ the corner shop 22 to 26). All candidates share the `city` atlas
cells.

Scoring columns: look match to the bible, triangles vs tier floor/target, UV/atlas fit, effort (minutes), licence and
cost, repeatability. A 1 to 5 score plus a sentence each; the owner's taste is recorded as a note, not a gate.

**Where files go**: renders and notes in `bakeoff/`; scratch `.blend` outside git; nothing in `art/export/` (D3).

**Source 1 baseline**: current meshes are `dt_hatchback` 216 triangles, `dt_bench` 48, `dt_street_tree` 88,
`dt_fire_hydrant` 176, and `dt_corner_bakery` 200 / `dt_corner_shop_row` 432 for `shop_front`. Source 1 is an
upgraded version of each, made in Blender within the proposed floor/target.

## R-09: CC0 candidates and licence

**Decision**: Source 2 uses a CC0 pack from Kenney or Quaternius (City/Car/Nature kits). For each candidate: record the
pack, URL, licence text snapshot and download date in the report; verify "CC0 1.0"; no attribution is required but
the credit line is recorded anyway. Import in Blender, re-UV each face into one atlas cell/strip, one material, delete
embedded textures. Reject any with transparency or PBR requirements. Specific models are chosen at implementation after
**Owner** approval of the download (Principle XII).

**Why Kenney/Quaternius**: both publish CC0 low-poly city kits close to the intended look, and CC0 removes attribution
and hand-off risk.

**Alternatives**: Poly Haven (the Blender MCP libraries are off, and its models are realistic, not stylised); Sketchfab
(licences vary per model).

## R-12: Heavy-scene construction

**Decision**: `tests/studio/HeavyScene.server.luau`, cloned into the Play place like `BotRun`. It builds the scene
procedurally with `AssetService:CreateEditableMesh` and `CreateMeshPartAsync` (verified to work; a 5,000 triangle
sphere was made), so no upload or `Assets.luau` id is needed (D2).

Sizing starts from the 008 tier counts 480 / 386 / 260 / 204 / 56 and budgets 500 / 700 / 1500 / 3500 / 5000, which at
full budget is 1,894,200 triangles (tier 4 to 5: 994,000). That is above the platform's roughly 1,000,000 envelope, so
the full-budget scene is scaled by a utilisation factor u: 300k → u 0.158 (tier 4-5 set ≈ 157k), 500k → 0.264 (≈ 262k),
750k → 0.396 (≈ 394k), 1M → 0.528 (≈ 525k). Part count stays under 2,000 (the 1,850 plan limit) at every step, and 12
balls run via the existing `BotRun` pattern in `winter` and `city`.

**Rationale**: separates triangle load from part count; the previous ceilings were guesses.

**Alternatives**: scaling the real Downtown (rejected, would change content); uploading meshes (rejected, owner
hand-off).

## R-13: Sampling method

**Decision**: as in the 008 Gate 2 method: a client `RunService.Heartbeat` sampler logs one sample per second for 60 s
per step (`FpsStats` pattern). Seconds at exactly 15 fps are labelled throttled; the verdict uses the other seconds.
`workspace:GetRealPhysicsFPS()` is the physics rate and is not render evidence. Triangles come from mesh data because
Luau cannot read `RenderStatsTriangles`, `TriangleCount` or `DrawCallsCount` (D7); `Stats.PrimitivesCount` and
`InstanceCount` are recorded as context. The LibMP MicroProfiler (`rbx-perf-profiling`) can add GPU/CPU frame times
from a snapshot; it is an optional extra.

The Studio MCP cannot resize the viewport and Studio is not unthrottled by activating it. If all seconds are
throttled the run counts as one tooling failure; ask the owner once to keep Studio in front, then rerun (AGENTS.md).

## R-14: Ceiling derivation, N10 rewrite and phone ask

**Decision**: the ceiling is the highest step with ≥ 30 fps on unthrottled seconds (all 60 for a pass, no more than
the stated tolerance of low seconds), minus a default 20 % margin rounded down to 10,000 (D5). Two constants:
`UNCULLED_TRIANGLE_CEILING` and `CULLED_TRIANGLE_CEILING` (culled = tier ≥ 4 set per `cullTierDelta = 2`). The
`Downtown N10` test (lines 1451 to 1469; today it sums manifest triangles over `resolved.winter.placements` against 250000
and 100000) keeps its audit shape and swaps the literals for the new named constants. `03-city-level.md` lines 65 to 66
get the same numbers and a pointer to `playtest.md`. `Config.Budgets` is not edited.

**Phone reading**: the **Owner** is asked once for an early reading on the mid-range phone. The result, when it comes,
is an RC item and may lower the ceiling later by a one-line constant change. It does not block this feature.

**Rationale**: a measurement on the desktop Studio is an upper bound, which is why the margin and the phone reading
exist; the owner decided phone fps is a non-blocking RC check.

**Alternatives**: setting the ceiling at the highest passing step with no margin (rejected, desktop is an upper bound).
